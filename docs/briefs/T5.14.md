# Task T5.14: Pixel art for the remaining items

Context: Rise & Shine Bakery, a browser merge game (TypeScript, Vite, PixiJS, Vitest).
Read first: public/art/*.png (the 23 finished items: the style to match), src/data/items.json (chain colors, tiers), src/render/assets.ts (`assetUrl` picks a key's .png over its .svg automatically), src/render/art.test.ts, docs/briefs/working-rules.md.

Goal: Every item on the board is drawn in the game's pixel style, replacing the numbered placeholder squares.

Scott's decision: the final art is 16 × 16 pixel art PNGs, not the plan's flat vector SVGs. Model: Sonnet, with the PixelLab tools (`pixelart_workbench`). Starts any time; each batch is independent.

Files to create or edit: new PNGs in public/art/ only, named `<spriteKey>.png`. Do not edit any other file. Leave the .svg placeholders in place: art.test.ts checks every item has one, and the .png takes precedence at runtime.

## The style (from the finished art)

- 16 × 16 pixels, transparent background, the item filling about 12–14 pixels.
- A 1-pixel outline in ink `#3a2414` all the way round.
- Each chain's fills are built on its `color` from items.json: a mid tone, one lighter highlight on the top left, one darker shade on the bottom right. At most 5 colors per item plus the ink.
- **Tiers must read at a glance:** each step up a chain is bigger, fuller or more decorated than the last (more items in the stack, a garnish, a box, a bow).
- Generators read as objects that make things: a coop, a tin, a mill, a fridge. Tiers 2 and 3 add visible upgrades (a second roof, a gold trim, a bigger body) on the same silhouette.
